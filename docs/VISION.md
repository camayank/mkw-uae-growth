## The vision

Tasees is the one counterparty a founder needs to set up and stay compliant in the UAE. MKW Advisors sells at one price with one promise; a vetted partner network delivers; MKW controls scope, price, allocation, quality and payment; software makes that control cheap enough to scale and honest enough to trust.

The product is a managed marketplace, not a lead marketplace: the client never shops, haggles or chases, and the partner never sells. Over five years it becomes the operating system a UAE business keeps using after setup, because its compliance calendar, documents and history live there.

**Three promises.**

- **To the client:** one price, one accountable firm, evidence for every step, a guarantee when we are late, and nothing forgotten after setup.
- **To the partner:** steady work without selling, fair allocation on merit, payment within days of client approval, and a scorecard that is visible and explainable.
- **To MKW:** the relationship, the data and the margin stay with MKW; every number reconciles; nothing is sent, paid or filed without a person or a tested rule.

## The problem and why a managed marketplace

A founder setting up in the UAE faces forty-plus free zones, mainland licensing, visas, VAT and corporate tax registration, ESR, UBO, AML, bank onboarding and a calendar of renewals, each sold by a different firm at an opaque price. The typical experience is five quotes, three WhatsApp threads, a surprise government fee and a missed renewal a year later.

The existing models each fail one side:

| Model | What the client gets | What breaks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single firm | One contact | Narrow capability, capacity limits, price set by the firm's own cost |
| Lead marketplace | Many quotes | No accountability, no guarantee, price pressure degrades quality |
| Freelancer platform | Low price | No licences, no insurance, no continuity |
| Tasees (managed) | One price, one accountable firm, a guarantee, a calendar | MKW must make control cheap: that is what the software is for |

MKW already has the advisory relationship, the pricing knowledge (the Offer Builder sheet) and the partner contacts. Tasees turns that into a repeatable, auditable system so one ops person can run fifty engagements with the same control MKW applies to five today.

## Who it serves

Five roles inside the platform, each seeing a different slice of the same engagement.

| Role | Who | Sees and does | Never sees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Client | Founder, their finance or admin person; 1–2 users per organisation | Intake, scoped plan, offer, accept and pay, upload documents, approve steps, compliance calendar, invoices | Partner cost, margin, who else quoted |
| Partner | Admin and delivery staff of a partner firm | Quote requests, allocated jobs, evidence uploads, document requests, own payouts and scorecard | Client price, margin, other firms, client name before accepting |
| MKW Ops | Delivery and account team | Review scoped plans, pick quotes, build offers, allocate, check evidence, confirm obligation dates, run the exception queue | Margin |
| MKW Finance | Accountant or finance lead | Invoices, payments, credit notes, payout runs, bank file, credit decisions, accounting export, margin | — |
| Founder | The owner | Everything, plus settings, margin rules, fee and Arabic sign-offs, erasure, pilot go / no-go | — |

**Named accountable people outside the system.** The platform expects a person by name for: fee verification (monthly), scoping-rule and zone-data verification (monthly), legal (terms, Partner Network Agreement, PDPL notice, consent text), tax (disbursement treatment, invoice layout, corporate tax rules), security review before pilot, and the Arabic reviewer.

**Pilot minimum.** Founder, one ops person, one finance person (one human may hold two roles at first), three partner firms with one admin each, five clients.

## The client journey

The client should feel one thing throughout: *I always know what happens next and what Tasees needs from me.* Every client screen opens with that one next action.

1. **Arrive.** A conversation, not a form: three to five questions (activity, where, people, timeline, budget). The scoped plan builds on screen as they answer, with confidence in plain words ("confirmed", "we will verify this"). Consent to the PDPL notice is one clear step, versioned.
2. **Receive the plan.** A person at MKW has reviewed the AI and rule drafts before the client sees them. The plan reads as advice: what to set up, in which order, why, what is still uncertain.
3. **Receive the offer.** A proposal, not a table: scope in plain words, what is included, a timeline, fees separate from government fees, VAT shown, the guarantee explained, one accept button. Any UNCERTAIN fee is marked and explained. The offer expires on a visible date.
4. **Accept and pay.** Versioned terms, deposit by card link or bank transfer, confirmation within minutes, invoice with TRN.
5. **Follow delivery.** One timeline: intake → offer → deposit → steps → done. Each step shows its evidence when the partner uploads it and MKW has checked it. Document requests arrive as a checklist with drag-to-upload that validates the file before they leave the page.
6. **Approve.** One click per step with the evidence in front of them; silence for five working days approves automatically and the client is told that rule up front.
7. **Be compensated when we are late.** A delay is noticed by the system, explained by a person, and the guarantee credit appears on the next invoice without the client asking.
8. **Stay compliant.** Completion creates the compliance calendar. Each date is confirmed by a person before it shows; reminders at 30 and 7 days; "met" only with filing evidence MKW has checked. Tasees never files for them, and says so.
9. **Come back.** The next renewal, the next visa, the next entity: the history, documents and pricing are already there. Arabic throughout, once reviewed.

**Measures of the experience.** Time from intake to offer under 2 working days; offer-to-acceptance rate; client actions completed within 24 hours of request; zero "where is my…" messages per engagement; renewals captured before expiry.

## The partner journey

The partner should feel: *MKW Advisors brings me work I did not have to sell, pays on time, and tells me exactly where I stand.* Partners never see the name Tasees on its own; the relationship is with MKW Advisors Partner Network.

1. **Apply.** Firm details, licence, insurance, categories and rate card; documents uploaded once. MKW reviews, pilots, signs. Licence and insurance expiry are watched and the firm is told before anything lapses.
2. **Quote blind.** A quote request shows emirate, zone and services, not the client's name or anyone else's number. The partner quotes cost and duration; a quote is a commitment.
3. **Receive work.** Allocation on merit: scorecard, fairness cap (no firm above 35% of a category), timeouts. The job board shows each step with its evidence checklist and the date MKW expects it.
4. **Deliver with evidence.** Upload the licence copy, the visa stamp, the filing receipt; a checker flags a wrong document type or expired date before MKW sees it. Request documents from the client through MKW, never directly.
5. **Get paid.** Payout eligible the moment the client approves; released in the weekly run; the partner sees eligible, pending and paid with the invoice numbers.
6. **See the scorecard.** On-time 40, quality 20, responsiveness 15, compliance 15, volume 10, recomputed weekly, with the inputs shown so a low score is explainable and fixable.

**What the network gets over time.** Predictable volume, a rate card that is respected, no price war with other partners, a reputation inside the network that earns more work, and zero collections risk because MKW holds the client relationship and the money.

## How MKW runs it

MKW's job inside the product is judgement, not administration. The software queues, drafts, checks and reconciles; a person decides.

**Ops command centre.** Ops opens to one triage queue ordered by risk and SLA: scoped plans to review, quotes to pick, below-floor lines, evidence to check, UNCERTAIN dates to confirm, delays to attribute, documents overdue from clients, partners near a licence expiry. Every item carries a "why it is here" line, the AI's draft where one exists, and a one-click action. The queue is empty at the end of each working day; the morning digest says what came in overnight.

**Finance.** Invoices issue from milestones; payments match in from card webhooks and bank references; the weekly payout run produces a bank file and marks releases; credit decisions are a yes / no with the computed amount; the accounting export goes to the books monthly. Finance sees margin per line and the reconciliation between the dashboard and the ledger.

**Founder.** The dashboard by month, quarter or year: revenue, VAT, cash, payouts, margin, on-time rate, request-to-acceptance, partner share by category, scorecards. Settings hold the rules that need a signature: margin rates, fee verification, Arabic sign-off, retention, erasure. A founder analyst answers questions over the numbers with the rows cited.

**The operating rhythm.** Daily: digest and queue to zero. Weekly: scorecards, delays, credits, payout run. Monthly: fee and rule verification, accounting export, KNOWN-GAPS review. Quarterly: partner reviews and the roadmap.

## The intelligence layer

AI agents make MKW's control cheap; they never replace the person who is accountable. Every agent reads events, writes a draft into a review queue marked UNCERTAIN, and a named person approves, edits or rejects it. No agent can set a price, pick a partner, close a step, send a message, move money, file anything, or state a statutory conclusion to a client.

| Agent | Trigger | Drafts | Approved by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scoping | Intake submitted | Service plan with confidence per line and the questions to ask back | Ops |
| Document checker | Evidence or document uploaded | Type, names, dates, expiry, match to the step's requirement; flags gaps | Ops (one click) |
| Fee and rule watcher | Monthly, and on authority page change | Changed fee rows and obligation rules with the source quoted | Fee verifier / rule verifier |
| Compliance calendar | Engagement completed; licence documents read | Proposed obligations and dates | Ops |
| Drafting | Any ops action that sends text | Client and partner messages, offer cover notes, delay explanations, in both brands and both languages | Ops |
| Partner vetting | Application documents uploaded | Pre-filled expiry dates, gaps, risk notes | MKW reviewer |
| Risk scoring | Continuous | Engagement risk from late evidence, scorecard trend, document gaps, client silence; orders the ops queue | Nobody: it ranks, it never decides |
| Translation | New string or template | Arabic drafts | Arabic reviewer |
| Founder analyst | Question asked | Answer over the snapshot tables with rows cited; read-only | Founder / finance |

**The learning loop.** Every human edit to a draft is recorded: what the agent proposed, what the person changed, how long it took. That becomes a quality metric per agent (edit rate, acceptance rate) and a lowered confidence for an agent whose drafts are heavily edited, so the queue shows the person how much to trust each one. Over time the same record is the training set for better prompts and, later, fine-tuned models, without ever having let a model act alone.

**Boundaries.** Models run behind the `ai` adapter with a monthly spend cap; personal data sent to a model is minimised and logged; the in-memory double stays for tests; a model outage degrades to "no draft", never to a blocked workflow.

## Architecture vision

The shape built in O1–O9 stays; the next outcomes add the parts that make it stronger and smarter without changing the path a change takes.

People act through the API; every change lands in the audited database; adapters and agents only ever read from it and write drafts back for a person.

**What exists and stays.** pnpm monorepo, Node 22, TypeScript; `packages/core` pure domain with no I/O; Fastify API with zod; PGlite in development and CI, Postgres in production; three database roles with row-level security and column grants; hash-chained audit; adapters with in-memory doubles; one jobs runner; no-build web app; Playwright smoke with axe-core.

**What changes next.**

1. **Event outbox.** Domain events written in the same transaction as the change; a dispatcher delivers email, webhooks and agent work from the outbox with retries. Exactly-once side effects, replay, and a feed every agent can subscribe to.
2. **State machines as data.** Request, offer, engagement, milestone, invoice, payout and obligation transitions declared as tables of (from, action, role, guard) in core, applied by one `transition()`. Screens can ask "what can happen next" generically.
3. **Route modules and OpenAPI.** `server.ts` split by domain with schemas exported; a generated spec gives typed clients and a contract test.
4. **Idempotency keys** on every mutating route and job, so a retry never double-creates.
5. **Read models.** Daily snapshots per client, partner and category for dashboards, exports and the analyst; transactional tables stay lean.
6. **Observability.** Structured logs with user, org and request id; job metrics; alerts when a job did not run; `verify:audit` nightly.
7. **Real Postgres in CI** beside PGlite; a load test on allocation and payout before the pilot; one validated config schema at boot.
8. **Web app modules.** One module per screen over a small shared component layer (table, form, status chip, timeline, money) and design tokens; still no build step.

## Design principles for the UI and UX

1. **One next action.** Every screen for a client or partner opens with the single thing we need from them, then the context. If there is nothing to do, say so.
2. **Status you can see at a glance.** A timeline, not a table, for the engagement; a queue, not a list, for ops; a proposal, not a grid, for the offer.
3. **Money in plain sight, by role.** Fees, government fees and VAT always separated; an UNCERTAIN item always carries one sentence saying why and what happens next. What a role must not see is absent, not greyed.
4. **Conversation over forms.** Intake asks a few questions and shows the plan building; document requests are checklists with validation before upload leaves the page.
5. **Say the rule before it bites.** Offer expiry, five-working-day auto-approval, the guarantee, "we do not file for you": stated where the client acts, in their language.
6. **Two brands, one system.** Tasees to clients, MKW Advisors to partners; the same components, different voice and wordmark; no screen ever shows both.
7. **Arabic is first-class.** Logical layout, numbers and references left-to-right, reviewed wording only; English until a reviewer signs.
8. **Accessible by construction.** WCAG 2.1 AA checked by machine on every screen in both languages; keyboard path for every client action; focus and announcements on every route change.
9. **Phone is a real layout.** Clients approve steps and upload documents from a phone; those paths are designed at 390 px first.
10. **Design tokens, few components.** One type scale, spacing scale, colour roles and elevation; a dozen shared components; no framework, no build step.
11. **Quiet by default.** No marketing surfaces inside the app; one notification per event; the digest, not a stream of emails, for ops.

## Rules that never bend

The vision depends on these; a change that weakens one is not shipped, whatever else it adds.

1. **Privacy of money.** Clients never see partner cost, margin or payouts; partners never see client price, margin or other quotes; ops never sees margin. Enforced in the database, the visibility map and tests.
2. **One margin rule** prices offers, change orders and re-allocations; below the floor goes to review; a partner is never paid above the cost the price was built on.
3. **Integer fils and basis points.** VAT 5% on fees; government fees at cost, no VAT.
4. **Done means evidenced.** Evidence, MKW check, client approval or the tested five-working-day rule.
5. **Uncertain is a state.** Unverified fees, rules, dates and AI output are UNCERTAIN until a named person approves. No automatic statutory conclusions or filings.
6. **No unattended side effects.** Nothing sent, paid, released or filed without a person or an explicit, tested rule.
7. **Adapters with doubles.** Tests never touch the internet.
8. **Append-only audit**, hash-chained; erasure tombstones personal data elsewhere.
9. **UAE PDPL.** Consent versioned; data in a UAE region; masking between parties; export and erasure keeping what tax law requires; no scraped personal contacts.
10. **Branding.** "Tasees, by MKW Advisors" to clients; "MKW Advisors" to partners, never Tasees alone.
11. **Dependencies** need a DECISIONS entry and a pinned version.
12. **Extend, never rewrite.** Working code and passing tests are assets.

## Business model and the numbers that matter

MKW earns the margin between the partner's cost and the client's price on professional fees; government fees pass through at cost. The platform exists to earn that margin at scale without losing control.

**How a line is priced.** Price = max(cost × (1 + rate), cost + floor), rounded up to AED 50, capped at 1.25 × market mid. Rates and floors per category are the owner's decision (seeded at 20% and AED 300–500, marked "default, confirm"). VAT 5% on fees. Deposit 50% of fees plus all government fees. Guarantee 10% of a line's fee per step more than five working days late.

**Numbers the founder watches, all on the dashboard.**

| Measure | Why it matters | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin achieved vs rule | Whether ops is holding the price | invoices and engagement lines, finance and founder only |
| Request-to-acceptance rate | Whether the offer sells | requests and offers by period |
| Time intake → offer | Ops speed; target under 2 working days | request and offer timestamps |
| On-time rate | The promise kept; drives credits | milestones vs expected dates |
| Credits issued | Cost of lateness | guarantee credits |
| Partner share by category | Fairness cap and concentration risk | allocations |
| Payouts paid vs invoices | Cash discipline | payout runs and invoices |
| Ops minutes per engagement | What the agents are for | queue timings (O10) |
| Renewals captured | The second sale | obligations met through Tasees |

**Unit economics to prove in the pilot.** Margin per engagement after credits; ops time per engagement; partner payout cycle; cost of AI drafts per engagement against the ops time saved.

## Risks and what we will not do

| Risk | Control |
| --- | --- |
| A wrong statutory date or fee reaches a client | UNCERTAIN state, named verifier, the label stays on the client's screen, reminders say "check with MKW" |
| Money leaks between parties | Three enforcement layers and tests that read every forbidden field as every role |
| Partners paid outside the platform | Scorecards, fairness cap and books all break at once; the pilot rule is no side deals, and the dashboard shows the gap |
| An agent acts alone | Agents write drafts only; no agent can send, pay, allocate, close or file, and the tests prove it |
| A quiet job failure | Observability outcome before pilot: alerts when a job did not run, nightly audit verification |
| Skipping the security review because tests are green | Tests prove the rules as written; a reviewer looks for the rules nobody wrote |
| Arabic wording about money or obligations is wrong | Arabic reaches clients only after a reviewer signs; numbers are never translated |
| Personal data handled carelessly | PDPL consent versioned, UAE region, masking, export and erasure, no scraped personal contacts |
| Rewriting working code for taste | Extend, never rewrite; one outcome at a time with a contract |

**What we will not do.** Sell leads or expose partners to clients directly. Let a model file, pay, price or conclude. Show a client a number nobody has verified. Build a feature without an outcome contract and evidence. Add a dependency without a decision. Use the name Tasees to partners. Launch without the lawyer, the tax adviser and the security reviewer.
